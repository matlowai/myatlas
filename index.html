Topography & bathymetry

The surface balances uplift, the buoyancy of crust, and erosion. Colours show height above or below sea level. Shading makes the relief easier to read; it does not change the elevations.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

The planet starts with declared crust and a pattern of moving plates. As the simulation advances, stretching, collision and erosion reshape that starting surface.

Where it is going

Finer terrain will connect the planetary picture to the ground around a traveller. More detail must follow the same geology and drainage.

Plates & boundaries

Plates carry parcels of crust across a sphere. Their relative motion identifies spreading ridges, subduction, collision and sideways slip. Motion is prescribed by this model rather than solved from a full mantle flow.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

A starting arrangement of plates and crust provides the initial conditions. Saved frames record how the arrangement changes over four hundred million model years.

Where it is going

More frequent saved frames will make the history easier to follow. Stronger links between motion, crust formation and rock history will make the consequences more complete.

Volcanoes, arcs & hotspots

The records distinguish volcanic arcs, hotspots, rifts and broad volcanic provinces. Symbols mark recorded vents, including submerged ones. Their size is a map convention, not the width of a volcano.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

Volcanism begins in the settings supplied by plate motion and the model’s melt rules. The records retain onset and last-eruption times; a quiet vent still has a history.

Where it is going

Richer magma and eruption histories can connect material composition, landform shape and mineral formation. The map alone does not establish those missing chemical details.

Annual temperature

An energy-balance model combines sunlight, heat loss and transport toward the poles. Altitude cools the surface. This layer shows annual mean temperature calculated on the saved terrain, not daily weather.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

The climate calculation starts from the planet’s latitude, elevation and land–ocean pattern. It was calculated after this geological run, so it does not recover the climate in which old rocks formed.

Where it is going

Seasonal and formation-time climate records will help explain weathering, sediment and vegetation through time.

Annual precipitation

Moisture moves through the atmosphere, gains water from evaporation, and loses it as rain. Rising air over mountains encourages rainout. The colour scale is logarithmic so dry and wet regions can both be read.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

The saved climate calculation uses the terrain and ocean pattern. The older erosion calculation used uniform runoff, so these rainfall colours did not drive the rivers shown here.

Where it is going

Using the same rainfall history for erosion and deposition will connect these maps causally, rather than merely placing them side by side.

Aridity & drylands

Dryness depends on both water supply and drying demand. The aridity index divides precipitation by potential evapotranspiration: how much water the atmosphere could remove. Small values indicate drylands.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

The index is derived from the saved annual climate. Where drying demand is zero, the ratio is undefined and is labelled separately; an icy region is not automatically a desert in this classification.

Where it is going

Seasonal water budgets and recorded past climates can improve the connection to soils, vegetation and climate-sensitive mineral formation.

Rivers & receivers

Each river segment follows a recorded downstream receiver. Water collects as paths join, and the line styling indicates a runoff-based discharge estimate. Rivers belong to the drainage network rather than a drawn guide line.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

This network was solved on a coarse grid with uniform runoff. Its regular patterns reflect cells about seventy kilometres across. It is not a fine-scale map of river bends.

Where it is going

Connected drainage at finer scales, flow-driven bank erosion, deposition and cutoff processes will bring the network closer to the rivers experienced on the ground.

Drainage basins

A basin collects water toward a shared outlet. Colours distinguish basin identities, not rainfall amounts. The labels use non-marine catchment area and the saved outlet records.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

Basins arise from the terrain and its downstream connections. The model records supported sea and lake terminals; a drainage path does not have to end in the sea.

Where it is going

Finer terrain must preserve connected flow, declared outlets and water balance. Changing a divide should be a modeled event with consequences downstream.

Exposed lithology

Lithology means rock type. Each cell shows the first recorded layer at least one metre thick. The result distinguishes broad volcanic, sedimentary and other rock classes; it is not a detailed survey of outcrops.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

Rock columns carry the effects of formation, deposition, burial and erosion. The map reads those columns at the saved present day, including declared material from before the simulated interval.

Where it is going

More complete formation and pressure–temperature histories will connect broad rock classes to detailed underground structure and fresh exposures.

Explore this layer ↗

Surface rock age

Surface rock age measures time before the final snapshot, using the midpoint of the exposed layer’s formation interval. An interval is an uncertainty range, not an exact birthday.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

Some starting crust is older than the four-hundred-million-year run. Those older ages are initial conditions; they are not evidence that all of that earlier history was simulated.

Where it is going

Keeping formation, burial and exposure histories distinct will make it possible to explain why old rock is exposed at a young landscape surface.

Explore this layer ↗

Mineral provinces

Mineral provinces group connected geological source support over twenty-million-year intervals. The colours show the latest recorded membership of each crust parcel at its present position. They do not show ore grade or a mining boundary.

How it started · where it is going

How it started

The map is derived from retained volcanic and material history. Older memberships can overlap. Conditional mineral initialization uses finite source material; it is not a complete simulation of mantle chemistry and circulating fluids.

Where it is going

Formation history, finite mineral transfers and mining should tell one consistent story. Better fluid and chemical histories will replace conditional assumptions where the model is incomplete.

From a planet to a place

The ground has a past.
And a future.

A world that arrives around you

The game builds nearby terrain as a traveller moves through one generated world. Local rock and terrain detail come from the same geological products as the planetary view. Places are reached through continuous travel.

How it started

A global record provides the context from which local surfaces and material histories can be derived.

Where it is going

Continuous refinement will connect the wide view to the ground beneath the player, with consistent geology across the journey.

Destruction and mining

The whole world is intended to be blastable, mineable and destructible. Every request derives the terrain and material it needs at that place. A missing material rule must be explained as missing data, never disguised as a geographic permission boundary.

How it works

Breaking rock changes its form and location. Mining transfers finite material into collected lots while retaining its origin. Independent review checks proposed changes, and geometry is published before the authoritative world accepts them. New floating-point transfers conserve material within a stated tolerance of about 0.01% per mineral.

How it started

The foundation is shared geological source material with stable identity and a record of accepted changes. A source body enters the accounting record once, when first needed.

Where it is going

Terrain, fresh cuts, rubble, inventory and readable mineral origins must work together in the game. This atlas presents the concept and geological context; it is not a playable mining demonstration or proof of complete planet coverage.

Lessons from building a simulated planet

Make the explanation
as honest as the picture.

Resolution is a promise

A smooth image is not a finer simulation. State the native cell size, distinguish display interpolation from modeled detail, and show what remains unresolved.

Connected models need shared causes

Climate, rivers, sediments and resources should exchange consistent histories. Two attractive maps do not prove that one actually drove the other.

Keep assumptions visible

Declared starting conditions and reduced models make large simulations practical. Give them units, validity limits and a clear path to replacement when better evidence arrives.

Test the result people use

Check a real interaction and inspect the resulting image. Use focused conservation and connection checks where they protect the model; add deeper tests when a real failure calls for them.

Keeping a long project pointed the same way

Carry the intent
with the work.

A decision ledger

Give decisions stable identifiers, explicit scope, dependencies and acceptance criteria. Keep the reasoning and evidence findable across handoffs. Changed decisions retain a visible history.

Rules travel with every task

Attach applicable constraints to each assignment and resume. Name the owned files, inputs, expected output and unresolved assumptions so the next person starts with the same boundaries.

Review before merging

Read the actual changes, exercise the intended behavior and inspect the evidence before integrating the result. Agreement alone is not acceptance.

Mechanical checks support judgment

Check broken references, missing evidence and common rule violations automatically. Text matching cannot prove physical validity or completeness. Review must still follow the causal chain.

What these maps can tell you

Recorded, not reconstructed.

Data come from the simulated planet run and its saved climate and material-history calculations. The native globe has 576 × 288 cells, spaced 0.625° apart: about 70 kilometres north–south. Present-day display textures are 8192 × 4096 pixels; historical textures are 4096 × 2048. Zoom does not add simulated detail.

Climate, rivers, rock types, rock ages, volcano records and mineral provinces describe the final snapshot. Only six fields have saved frame history. The chart reads 200 recorded samples. Older rock ages include declared pre-run material. A mineral province is geological context, not a measured ore reserve.

Province numbers are public display identifiers derived from the saved catalogue, not names or new geological assignments. The underlying location, interval and class remain those of the recorded data.
